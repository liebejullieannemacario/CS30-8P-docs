Adaptive Traffic Control and Smart Energy Infrastructure

This is a project page for the class CS30-8P Websites and Online Communication for Mapua students of BS Technical Communication.

Executive Summary

Metro Manila faces heavy traffic congestion and high municipal electricity costs, but expanding roads is expensive and limited by land. Using existing infrastructure more intelligently through smart, data-driven technology is a practical, low-cost alternative.

1. The Problem: Static Signal Cycles

Most signalized intersections in Metro Manila run on fixed timers, giving equal green light time regardless of actual vehicle count. This wastes green time on empty approaches while causing severe gridlock and spillback on busy lanes.

2. The Solution & Energy Efficiency

We propose a modernization of current systems using the following technologies:

  • Adaptive Traffic Control: Camera-based sensors and edge processing units continuously measure queue lengths to adjust green phases dynamically.
  • Smart Streetlights: LED streetlamps equipped with motion and ambient sensors dim during low-traffic hours (11:00 PM to 4:00 AM) to cut LGU power expenses.
  • Solar Resilience: Solar battery backups ensure traffic signals remain safely operational during typhoons and grid power outages.

3. Privacy, Implementation & Governance

Addressing Data Privacy

Edge computing processes all video locally on the camera device. Only numerical counts (e.g., "12 vehicles") leave the system. No raw video streams are transmitted, ensuring strict compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173).

Implementation Steps

  1. Start with small pilots at high-congestion intersections.
  2. Expand to full corridors.
  3. Secure sustained funding and coordinate between LGUs and national agencies.

Key Terminology

Edge Computing
Processing data locally on the device rather than sending it to a remote cloud server.
LGU
Local Government Unit.
MMDA
Metropolitan Manila Development Authority.
